◈ MISSION BRIEF
Firewall egocast the team to Gutenberg Station following a distress call from an embedded asset. They arrived just before all outside comms were severed. A TITAN fetch has taken control and is building something inside the processor locus — something that must not be allowed to finish.
◈ PRIMARY OBJECTIVE Identify and neutralize the fetch. Destroy or disable the processor locus before its construction completes. Survival is secondary to mission success — this may be a one-way trip.
◈ PC CONSTRAINTS PCs arrive as infomorphs instantiated in digimorphs with copylock active. They cannot fork, switch home devices, or access their gear — only their muses. The Firewall-friendly operator left a backdoor code and an exploit app as a welcoming gift. Everything the PCs need to fight back must be found or fabbed on station.
WHAT IS COPYLOCK?  Copylock is standard meshware that Nimbus installs on all rented digimorphs — the digital equivalent of a hotel room key that only works while you're checked in. It prevents an infomorph ego from being copied, forked, or moved to a different home device without Nimbus authorisation. Under normal circumstances it releases automatically during onboarding within minutes of arrival. The fetch took control of the station before that process completed. The PCs are stuck inside morphs they don't fully own, tethered to a home device on a compromised server, with no ability to duplicate themselves or run to safety. It is the session's opening cage — everything changes the moment it breaks.

STRUCTURE
A vertical bore approximately 1.2m wide running through the asteroid's long axis from the Level 1 transit hub junction (~0m) down to the locus bulkhead (~120m). Anchor rungs set into both walls at 1m intervals — the standard beehive hand-over-hand configuration. Wide enough for one person plus kit, or cargo movement with both walls used. Not wide enough for two people to pass without one anchoring flat to the wall. Lighting: emergency amber strips on 14-day battery backup — failing in sections by Day 4. Cargo guide rails run along the right wall — the remnants of a magnetic sled system the fetch used to move equipment to the locus.
MOVEMENT MECHANICS
Free Fall governs all shaft movement. No gravity — every direction is equally valid. Without the skill, characters grab rungs and pull themselves along at roughly walking pace (~4m per action turn). With Free Fall 40+, push-off-and-glide between anchor points is efficient and quiet. A full traverse of the shaft (120m) takes approximately 3 minutes at cautious pace, 90 seconds at speed. Speed sacrifices Infiltrate entirely — a gliding character bounces off walls and generates noise audible 20m away in pressurised sections. Bot ②'s advantage: it can anchor with four manipulator arms simultaneously and has 360° vision — it doesn't need to face the direction of threat, and it cannot be flanked.
NOISE & DETECTION
In pressurised atmosphere, metal-on-metal contact in the shaft is audible. Bot ②'s audio sensors cover the full shaft length. Relevant tests:

Careful movement (Infiltrate): opposed by Bot ②'s Perceive 40. Success = undetected approach. Failure = bot alerted, minion AI activates the other two bots within 30 seconds.
Fast movement (no Infiltrate possible): automatic detection if Bot ② is in the shaft. No roll — the noise is simply audible. Only viable if the bot has been disabled, jammed, or distracted.
Atmosphere status: If sections of the shaft have lost pressure (Day 4+ or deliberate depressurisation), sound transmission in those sections drops to zero. A vacuum section in the shaft is tactically useful — movement through it is silent regardless of skill. Vacsuits required to survive it.
LIGHT & VISIBILITY
Emergency lighting is failing progressively. By Day 4, approximately 60% of the shaft is dark — alternating lit and unlit sections. Characters without Enhanced Vision or a light source operate at −20 to Perceive and Infiltrate in dark sections. Bot ② has standard sensor package including Lidar — it does not need light to perceive. A light source helps the team but does not help them hide. Enhanced Vision ware or thermographic sensors are the correct tools here — see the fabber's available blueprints for thermal goggles (Moderate complexity, 2-hour print).
THE CARGO SLED
A magnetic cargo sled runs on guide rails along the shaft's right wall — used by the fetch's minion AIs to move fabrication equipment to the locus in the hours after takeover. It's still on the rails, parked approximately 40m down the shaft. Capacity: roughly 200kg of cargo, or one person plus kit. Movement: rail-guided, electrically powered, quiet — significantly quieter than free movement since it runs on rails rather than kicking off walls. Tactical use: the sled can carry a physical team member down the shaft past Bot ②'s position if the bot has been disabled, jammed, or its radio severed. Running the sled with an active bot in the shaft is a gamble — the rails run directly past the bot's patrol position. Interface 40 to operate the sled controls. Risk: the sled announces its approach from the moment it powers up — the motor hum is audible to the bot 60m+ away, giving it time to prepare an ambush at the sled's only stopping point (the locus bulkhead).
BOT ② — SHAFT PATROL SPECIFICS
Patrols a 30m section of the shaft centred at approximately the 60m mark — the midpoint between the Level 1 junction and the locus bulkhead. Patrol cycle: 4 minutes per pass. At any given moment there is a 2-minute window when the bot is at the far end of its patrol range (either near Level 1 junction or near the locus approach) where a fast-moving character could theoretically pass its usual position undetected. This requires both timing knowledge (Perceive 50 to establish the patrol pattern from a safe observation position) and speed (Free Fall 60 to transit the 30m danger zone in the window). The safer route: disable the bot first. The shaft becomes trivial without it.
SECONDARY ACCESS SHAFTS
Comms shaft (Level 1 → Level 2): 25m upward branch, accessible from the transit hub left wall. Narrower than the main shaft — one person at a time, no cargo clearance. No bot patrol. Access to Level 2 relay/comms room without entering the main shaft. Structural condition: intact.

No bypass to Level 3 exists. The main shaft is the only route to the locus bulkhead. The asteroid's rock composition at this depth is a dense metallic nickel-iron matrix — cutting a new route requires industrial drilling equipment not available on station. Characters who want to reach the locus must go through the shaft, go through the virtual approach (Path B), or destroy it from outside (Path C demolition charges placed at the bulkhead exterior via the main shaft — same problem).

◈ GM NOTES — RUNNING THIS SCENARIO
This scenario works because the PCs are powerless in the usual way — no bodies, no weapons, no morphs — and must fight using intelligence, creativity, and the tools they find. The horror is the stillness: a station that was full of people an hour ago is now empty and silent except for three murder robots and something building itself in the dark. Lean into the CCTV footage. Make the players watch what happened. Then make them figure out what to do next with no resources and a shrinking air supply.
The scenario has a clear progression: (1) find out what happened → (2) find the drone → (3) fix the drone → (4) secure the cable/gray box → (5) bridge the airgap while the bots try to stop you. Don't rush the middle steps. The investigation phase is where the horror lands. The drone operation is where the tension peaks. The resolution is where the philosophy lives.
Track the life support timer loosely — it's flavor pressure, not a hard kill timer. The goal is urgency, not punishment.
Don't reveal the fetch's true purpose until the PCs get deep — ideally until they're either inside the VR or successfully hacking the locus. The mystery keeps them moving. When it lands, it should reframe everything they thought they understood about the mission. The best reveals make the players go back and reread the evidence they already found.
If the players meet the imprisoned egos before choosing a resolution, they will face the weight of the physical destruction option. This is not a mistake to avoid — it's the scenario's moral core. Firewall's mandate is clear: neutralize the threat. What the players choose to do with the people in the way is between them and the table.
Let the consequences of their choice carry into future sessions. The uploaded stacks, the brinker community, the Nimbus operator's fate — none of these should just be forgotten.

Drone Whisperer trait lets R.A.I.D. spend Flex on Nova's automech tests — he can support all three other players simultaneously.
Copylock is the session's structural gear-shift. Everything before breaking it is constrained. Everything after it opens up. Don't let the PCs break it in the first 20 minutes — the tension of the locked state is the session's first act.
LOCKED
Home device = clinic server. One instance per PC. Minion AIs know exactly where to hit. No forking, no home-swap. This is the horror state.
BREAKING IT
Either brute Infosec at –60 (loud, risky, may trigger alert) or find disable codes in the security server via admin access (the intended path — requires backdoor code first). Flash's recognition of the encryption type points R.A.I.D. to the right server node.
UNLOCKED
Forking opens. Home-swap possible. Minion AIs now have to track multiple instances. R.A.I.D. can run a distraction fork while hacking the locus. Luz can send a fork into VR alongside Flash. The fetch's tactical advantage shrinks significantly — which is why it'll respond hard the moment copylock breaks.
