◊ SITUATION BRIEF
The team returns to Vesta in the wake of Gutenberg — back in their own morphs, MESA's device in hand, looking for a quiet handoff and a way out of the Belt. They don't get it. Project Ozma's net is already closing: fabricated rep deposits have poisoned their standing with Firewall, a closure team is moving through Vesta's Docking Ring District, and Lixia Lok — last seen in Binge — is already on station running point for the extraction. The session is a race: get to their ship, get MESA out, and break burn before the net pulls tight.
◊ WHAT THE TEAM KNOWS ON ARRIVAL Firewall has gone cold. No handler contact. Rep deposits in all accounts — identical amounts, simultaneous — flagged by Firewall's automated monitoring. They are persons of interest. They are not formally detained. Yet. MESA's device is with them and they know Ozma wants it.
◊ WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING Lok is coordinating a three-stage closure: a Firewall-adjacent hold order to freeze the team's transit credentials, a physical acquisition team in the Docking Ring to intercept them before they reach their berth, and a contingency extraction that activates if the team breaks through the first two layers. Ozma wants MESA's data. Firewall has been fed enough fabricated evidence that it won't intervene — it's watching.
◊ PC WIN CONDITION Reach their ship with MESA's device intact. Break Vesta orbit before the hold order activates their berth lockout. Expose or evade Lok without losing MESA to the extraction team.

This session runs on momentum and environmental awareness, not interrogation. The PCs are in familiar territory, moving fast, with a clock running. Every scene should feel like it has a pace — Vesta's microgravity is the texture, not a mechanic. The twelve-hour window is generous if the team is decisive. It becomes tight if they stop to have long conversations or freeze at decision points.
This session is designed to center FLASH. The maintenance tunnel route, the low-g traversal advantages, the collar release sequence, the knowledge of the ring — these are all FLASH moments. Present them proactively. Don't make these rolls — make them facts.
The exception is when FLASH's knowledge gaps matter narratively. Pick one moment of FLASH uncertainty per session, not five.
Lok's intercept scene is the session's narrative peak, not its action peak. The action is the escape. Lok is the moment where the team understands what they escaped from — and who is still watching.
If the team takes Lok's deal: clean exit, but Ozma has the coordinates and the team is in small debt to an Ozma asset. If they refuse: messier departure but MESA completely clean. If they fly past without engaging: the rep cloud persists into Signal Zero. All three are valid.
The session ends with the ship in burn toward the outer system. MESA is reactivated in transit. Her first words close this session and open the next. Let the transit be peaceful. Let the players breathe. Then let MESA ask her question.
MESA's question — "Is the signal still active?" — is the campaign's through-line. The answer, as far as anyone knows: yes. It has been active since before any of them were born.
◊ THE SEMAPHORE — BURIED OZMA FILE
Lok's primary objective is MESA's Gutenberg signal data. Her secondary objective — the one she hasn't articulated to her assets — is ensuring Firewall's analysts never see the secondary signal component of the Gutenberg transmission. Ozma's prior analysis contains a detail that has been buried internally for decades: the secondary signal came from sunward, not from beyond the ecliptic. Whatever sent the warning was already inside the solar system.
LOK'S SECONDARY MISSION
The true answer: COEWS-ANOMALY-7741. Ozma's internal designation for the sunward signal source. They have known for decades. They are not sharing.
THE BURIED DETAIL: The secondary signal in the Gutenberg transmission did not originate from outside the ecliptic. It came from the direction of the Sun. Ozma confirmed this and filed it under a restricted access header: COEWS-ANOMALY-7741. COEWS stands for Composite Orbital Early Warning System. Ozma knows what the Semaphore is. They have known for decades.
MESA — IF REACTIVATED AND ASKED
There's a file reference in what I could access of Ozma's query logs — the header COEWS-ANOMALY-7741. COEWS is a Foundation-era acronym. Composite Orbital Early Warning System. The secondary signal in the Gutenberg data came from sunward. Not from outside the ecliptic — from inside it. Whatever sent the warning was already in the solar system.
