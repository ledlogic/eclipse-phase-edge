◈ SITUATION BRIEF
The team returns to Vesta in the wake of Gutenberg — back in their own morphs, MESA's device in hand, looking for a quiet handoff and a way out of the Belt. They don't get it. Project Ozma's net is already closing: fabricated rep deposits have poisoned their standing with Firewall, a closure team is moving through Vesta's Docking Ring District, and Lixia Lok — last seen in Binge — is already on station running point for the extraction. The session is a race: get to their ship, get MESA out, and break burn before the net pulls tight.
◈ WHAT THE TEAM KNOWS ON ARRIVAL Firewall has gone cold. No handler contact. Rep deposits in all accounts — identical amounts, simultaneous — flagged by Firewall's automated monitoring. They are persons of interest. They are not formally detained. Yet. MESA's device is with them and they know Ozma wants it.
◈ WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING Lok is coordinating a three-stage closure: a Firewall-adjacent hold order to freeze the team's transit credentials, a physical acquisition team in the Docking Ring to intercept them before they reach their berth, and a contingency extraction that activates if the team breaks through the first two layers. Ozma wants MESA's data. Firewall has been fed enough fabricated evidence that it won't intervene — it's watching.
◈ PC WIN CONDITION Reach their ship with MESA's device intact. Break Vesta orbit before the hold order activates their berth lockout. Expose or evade Lok without losing MESA to the extraction team.

◈ VESTA DOCKING RING — OPERATIONAL AREA
The team's ground: Vesta's Docking Ring District — familiar from Session 03. The outer ring of Vesta's hab structure, dense with commercial berths, maintenance bays, transit hostels, and the kind of businesses that exist because ships stop here. High foot traffic. Multiple redundant access corridors. Low-g throughout — Vesta's gravity is barely 0.025g, meaning jumping the wrong way across a corridor costs seconds the team doesn't have.
FLASH knows this district. The team spent time here in the run-up to Gutenberg — sourcing morphs, making contacts, moving through the maintenance tunnels. That familiarity is now an operational edge. Lok's team is good, but they're working from surveillance data. FLASH has walked these corridors.
VESTA LOW-G ENVIRONMENT
The Docking Ring runs at roughly 0.025g — just enough to keep loose gear from floating away, not enough to run normally. Movement here is a skill. FLASH excels at this. Lok's acquisition team are belt-adapted but not ring-native. In a foot chase, the terrain favors whoever knows the angles. Use this. Jumps cover six to eight meters horizontally. The ceiling is four meters. The walls are curved.

REN ACHEBE
PROJECT OZMA FIELD OPERATIVE — ASSET ALPHA — FLASH CONNECTION
One of the two acquisition assets on Lok's closure team. Belt-native, non-binary, mid-thirties apparent age, currently sleeved in an unremarkable worker-caste splicer with the slightly-too-clean hands of someone who doesn't actually work docks. FLASH knows this face — not from Firewall, not from any sanctioned operation, but from a job they both did for the same employer without either knowing the other had been hired. A Belt gangster named Cass Voronova ran them as parallel bodyguards for three weeks across four stations, each convinced they were the primary protection. They weren't. They were the performance — two credible hard contacts circling Voronova at all times while the real business happened in rooms just out of their eyeline, with g-rep contacts who wouldn't have sat still if Voronova hadn't looked sufficiently insulated. Flash and Ren figured this out at roughly the same time, on the last station, after the job was done and the credits had cleared. They never saw Voronova again. They had a drink about it. That was three years ago.
FLASH CONNECTION PRIOR JOB: VORONOVA CIRCUIT BELT-NATIVE OZMA ASSET STUNNER ARMED
Cass Voronova was a mid-level Belt g-rep fixer — not a crime boss in any grand sense, more a specialist in high-margin short-window deals that required the kind of social credibility that only comes from being visibly hard to get to. The Voronova Circuit was three weeks, four stations: Ceres, Pallas, a mining platform in the outer Main Belt called Sard-7, and Vesta. Each stop involved Voronova meeting with g-rep representatives of various organisations under the cover of legitimate freight brokering. Each meeting required the attending parties to feel that approaching Voronova carried a physical cost — that the muscle around her was real, capable, and attentive.
THE SETUP
Flash was hired through a Belt security broker — a legitimate middleman who placed independent operators on short-term protection contracts. The brief was straightforward: travel with the client, look capable, respond to threats. Good money. Clean paperwork. Flash was told they were the primary protection and that a second operator would be present for "logistical support." Ren was told exactly the same thing in mirror image. Neither was told the other's name or background. They met at the first station handshake and immediately read each other as competitors for the same role. Voronova watched this dynamic develop and did nothing to correct it.
HOW VORONOVA USED THEM
During every substantive meeting, Voronova kept Flash and Ren visibly occupied: stationed at entrances, running counter-surveillance, managing arrival logistics. Anything that looked like real protection work and kept both of them in positions where they could see Voronova but not hear her conversations. Meanwhile Voronova was negotiating deals with g-rep contacts — organisations that would not have extended trust to an uninsulated fixer. The two credible hard presences circling Voronova at all times were the insulation. The deals closed. Voronova paid both contractors in full, thanked them professionally, and disappeared. She got what she came for. So did Flash and Ren. Nobody was cheated in any technical sense. They were just props.
THE LAST NIGHT — VESTA
The final station was Vesta. After the last handoff, Flash and Ren both clocked the shape of what had happened at roughly the same moment — comparing notes on what they'd each been doing during meetings and realising neither had any idea what the meetings had actually been about. The specific feeling of being professionally outmanoeuvred by someone who had used their own competence against them. They had a drink in a Docking Ring bar — this bar, on this station — and arrived at a mutual assessment: Voronova was good. They weren't angry. They were impressed, in the guarded way of people who respect the move even while resenting being the pieces. They parted with no obligations and no mesh IDs exchanged. Just a shared joke that only made sense to the two of them, and the understanding that if they ever crossed paths again it wouldn't be as strangers.
WHAT REN WAS ACTUALLY DOING
Ren was already an Ozma operative when they took the Voronova contract. The contract was sanctioned — Ozma wanted eyes on one of the g-rep contacts Voronova was meeting, and the bodyguard position gave Ren passive access to that contact's travel pattern and security posture. Flash's presence was incidental — a genuine independent hire that Voronova had made separately and that Ren had to work around. The fact that Flash was professionally competent, adaptable, and morally uncomplicated about grey-market work was something Ren noted and filed. The Ozma dossier on Flash begins with that contract. Lok has read it.
WHAT FLASH DOESN'T KNOW
Flash knows they were used as window dressing by a smart fixer. They don't know Ren was running a parallel operation the entire time. They don't know there's an Ozma file on them that starts with the Voronova job. From Flash's perspective, Ren is a capable independent operator they once worked alongside, got played alongside, and had a drink with about it. Someone who knows what it feels like to be the prop in someone else's deal. That shared experience of being outmanoeuvred is the actual texture of the relationship — not mutual respect built through hardship, but mutual embarrassment that became respect because neither of them pretended it hadn't happened.
Voronova is not present in this session. She is a ghost in the backstory — the person who set Flash and Ren's dynamic in motion without either of them knowing. Whether she ever becomes a live character is a campaign decision. What exists now: she is somewhere in the Belt, running the same kind of operation, and she is very good at reading people's professional pride and using it against them. If the GM ever wants to bring her in, she already has detailed files on both Flash and Ren's capabilities, habits, and blind spots.
The one live thread from the Voronova job: one of the g-rep contacts she was meeting had Ozma adjacency. That contact is in Ren's file. Flash doesn't know this exists. But if the campaign ever surfaces the Voronova connection in a context where Ozma's prior Belt network becomes relevant — sessions 07 onward, post-Signal Zero — the Voronova job is already a documented node.

◈ GM NOTES — RUNNING THIS SCENARIO
This session runs on momentum and environmental awareness, not interrogation. The PCs are in familiar territory, moving fast, with a clock running. Every scene should feel like it has a pace — Vesta's microgravity is the texture, not a mechanic. Use it constantly: things float, jumps cover unexpected distances, a push in the wrong direction costs precious seconds. The Docking Ring is a labyrinth that FLASH knows better than anyone coming after them.
The GM discipline here is keeping the clock present without making it punitive. The twelve-hour window is generous if the team is decisive. It becomes tight if they stop to have long conversations, run full investigations, or freeze at decision points. Keep the watcher's mesh ping in the back of their minds — every minute they spend in one place is a minute Lok's team is repositioning.
This session is designed to center FLASH. The maintenance tunnel route, the low-g traversal advantages, the collar release sequence, the knowledge of the ring — these are all FLASH moments. Present them proactively. When the team reaches the Transit Hub, FLASH should already know the ceiling traversal route. When they need the maintenance access code, FLASH has it. Don't make these rolls — make them facts.
The exception is when FLASH's knowledge gaps matter narratively — if the code has cycled, that creates genuine tension. Pick one moment of FLASH uncertainty per session, not five. The contrast between capable-and-certain and uncertain-and-pressured is what makes the uncertain moment land.
Lok's intercept scene is the session's narrative peak, not its action peak. The action is the escape. Lok is the moment where the team understands what they escaped from — and who is still watching. Her offer (pull the hold order in exchange for a data copy) is genuine and she means it. Whether they take it, refuse it, or fly past her without a word says something about the team's relationship with ambiguity and expedience.
If the team takes Lok's deal: they get a clean exit. Ozma has the coordinates. The team is now in debt to an Ozma asset — a small debt, but a real one. If they refuse: the departure is messier but MESA is completely clean. If they fly past her without engaging: she files the secondary report and the rep cloud persists into Signal Zero. All three are valid. None ends the Lok thread.
The session ends with the ship in burn toward the outer system. MESA is reactivated in transit — her first words close this session and open the next. The jump from Vesta to Signal Zero is a jump in register: from physical chase and social pressure to the vast quiet of the outer system and something genuinely unknown at FK7. Let the transit be peaceful. Let the players breathe. Then let MESA ask her question.
MESA's question — "Is the signal still active?" — is the campaign's through-line. It connects Gutenberg (where they found her) to Signal Zero (where she points them) to everything that follows. The answer, as far as anyone knows: yes. It is still active. It has been active since before any of them were born.

Her secondary objective — the one she hasn't articulated to her assets — is ensuring Firewall's analysts never see the secondary signal component of the Gutenberg transmission.
