◈ SITUATION BRIEF
The team returns from the outer system carrying something most of the inner system doesn't know about yet — proof that a TITAN fetch was building something at Gutenberg, that the signal came from FK7, that the Lithohex exists, and that something ancient near the sun has been pointing at these threats for years. Firewall wants the report filed quietly. It wants nothing else to happen. It will not get what it wants.

Planetary Consortium ambassador Danzan Alciato is attending a Glitter Bloc product launch at Profunda — a subsurface beehive habitat on Saturn's moon Enceladus. Alciato carries encrypted headware containing the full operational plan for a Consortium shipyard at Albiorix, designed to project hypercorp military power into the outer system. That shipyard, if built, changes the strategic picture for everything the team has been working to prevent. The outer system stays dark and brinker and hard to surveil right up until the moment it doesn't.

The team is tasked to retrieve the shipyard plans. The method is their choice. The venue is a live-streamed invite-only soirée. Several hundred people are attending. The Glitter Bloc's security is contractually sovereign on site.
◈ PRIMARY OBJECTIVE: Extract the encrypted data module from Danzan Alciato's headware implant by any means available — social, technical, physical, or pharmaceutical. Do not create an incident that makes the news.

◈ SECONDARY OBJECTIVE: Identify what the Consortium already knows about the outer-system arc. Did Alciato's intelligence service pick up the Gutenberg signal? Do they know about FK7? About the Lithohex? The answers change how much time the team has before hypercorp assets move into the Kuiper.

◈ CAMPAIGN THREAD: Kyo has the decryption key for Alciato's data module. She's Oversight — not Consortium. If she gets the key out, it either exposes Alciato or it disappears into another agency's filing system. Deciding who gets the decryption key, and what gets done with the data, is the session's moral question.

Extropian — no laws, only contracts. Glitter Bloc owns the venue and runs its own security and judiciary on retainer. Guests are contractually bound by Glitter Bloc security dictums on entry. No station police, no outside jurisdiction. Glitter Bloc security is the only law in the room.

Live-streamed to the mesh. Several hundred guests — figureheads, celebrities, diplomats, and outer-rim personalities. Everyone is dressed to kill. Designer drugs circulating openly. Runway presentation, fork burlesque, dueling DJs. The event is an occasion as much as a venue.

Glitter Bloc private guards and drones. All diplomats present have small armed personal details. Overt violence triggers immediate Glitter Bloc neutralisation — combatants removed, event continues. The show must go on. Covert action is not their problem unless it becomes visible.
MESH
Open to all guests. VR pods installed for morph demo experiences — Riff-Raff has already compromised the pod session manager. The hack is three days old. It is not on Glitter Bloc's radar.

◈ EXTRACTION PATHS — HOW TO GET THE DATA
The encrypted data module is a headware implant in Alciato's cyberbrain. Kyo has the decryption key. The team needs both. These are the viable approaches — most can be combined.
SOCIAL
Favour-trading and Deceive. If the team can establish credibility with Alciato — posing as interested parties in the Volkov deal, as Consortium-friendly contractors, or as people with their own outer-system intelligence to sell — Alciato may disclose more than intended. Deceive at +20 after a Vox introduction. The goal isn't to get Alciato to hand over the module — it's to get them talking about the shipyard in enough detail to reconstruct intent without needing the raw data.
PHARMACEUTICAL
Via Vox. Compliance agent in Alciato's drink — Persuade tests against Alciato gain +20, and Alciato won't remember clearly what they disclosed. Requires getting close enough to administer it (Sleight of Hand or a cooperative bartender via Rep). The 0.113g gravity means metabolisation is unpredictable — the window is short and Kyo will notice Alciato behaving oddly.
VR — BRAINHACK
Via Riff-Raff's compromised pods. If the team identifies the VR hack (Research or Infosec vs Riff-Raff's installed Firewall), they can piggyback Alciato's pod session. Riff-Raff will be in the same instance — the team either works with the hacker (splitting the data or paying for access) or competes against them simultaneously inside VR. Brainhacking in VR: the standard rules apply but the session environment makes physical interruption impossible — Kyo can't pull Alciato out manually.
PHYSICAL
Data module extraction — requires incapacitation first. The module is a headware implant — removal requires either a Medicine: Biotech test (at –30 without tools) or a Hardware: Electronics test on the cyberbrain interface. Not possible without getting Alciato alone and unconscious. The burlesque malfunction window (Phase 5) is the only realistic physical extraction opportunity without creating an incident. Kyo will notice absence within 4 minutes.
DECRYPTION KEY — KYO
The session's moral question. The data module is encrypted — without Kyo's key, extracted data is unreadable. Kyo will not hand over the key without either: (a) evidence the team has legitimate Firewall sanction to act against Consortium interests, or (b) a genuine personal appeal that the shipyard will enable something catastrophic in the outer system. The team can show her what they found at FK7 and Arrokoth. They can tell her about the Lithohex. Whether that's enough depends on the roleplay and the roll.
FORKNAPPING
Last resort. Alciato's cyberbrain can be accessed to pull a fork — the fork can then be persuaded or psychosurgery'd for the information. Requires the physical access described above, plus Infosec vs Alciato's cyberbrain security. Creates a fork that Alciato will know exists. If the fork is not destroyed, Alciato will pursue recovery through every available channel. If it is destroyed, the team has committed a capital crime under most jurisdictions.

The moral question — does Kyo hand over the decryption key — lives in this conversation. Luz's ability to read Kyo's hidden bigotry (Kinesics vs Deceive) gives her a lever most players won't find. Use it carefully: the bigotry isn't a weapon, it's a flaw that complicates an otherwise sympathetic character.
NOVA
COUNTER-SURVEILLANCE / RIFF-RAFF. Nova's Situational Awareness and Perceive 60 make him the team's eyes on the room's mesh layer. He's the one who spots the VR pod hack before Phase 4 — the anomalous traffic signature from Riff-Raff's installed exploit reads to his military-trained threat pattern recognition. His Sharpshooter and Melee Mastery are background insurance, but his real role is information brokerage: he notices what everyone else misses, including the Halsema/Grix exchange and Wadada's sidebar with Alciato.
R.A.I.D.
MESH OPERATIONS / VR LAYER. Interface 80, Infosec 80, Research 80 — R.A.I.D. owns the technical layer of this session. Identifying Riff-Raff's VR hack, running counter-intrusion on the pod network, pulling Alciato's mesh history, and brainhacking from inside the VR instance if the team goes that route. His saucer and speck drones can provide physical surveillance without requiring presence. The gray box from the Gutenberg session is still in inventory — it can be used to create a covert mesh connection to Alciato's cyberbrain if physical proximity is achieved.
◈ THE SEMAPHORE — WHAT ALCIATO KNOWS
The encrypted data module contains the shipyard plans — but it also contains Alciato's intelligence summary on outer-system developments. If the team decrypts it, they find three items relevant to the campaign arc.
GUTENBERG REPORT
Consortium intelligence filed the Gutenberg incident as a brinker habitat catastrophe, cause unknown. The report notes unusual signal traffic in the weeks preceding the event but attributes it to a malfunctioning relay. No TITAN classification. No Firewall flag. The Consortium is three analytical steps behind.
FK7 — FLAGGED
A Consortium survey probe recorded the FK7 source object's transmission six months before the team arrived. The recording is in Alciato's intelligence annex. The Consortium doesn't know what it is — but they flagged it as a potential resource anomaly and assigned a survey mission. That mission is currently en route. ETA: fourteen weeks. If the team doesn't do something with this information, Consortium assets will reach FK7 first.
COEWS — PARTIAL
There is a single line in the intelligence annex that reads: "COEWS-ANOMALY-7741 — cross-reference Ozma file. Consortium assets have no access." That's it. That's all Alciato has on the Semaphore — a file number and the knowledge that Ozma has something they don't. They filed it and moved on. The team now knows that Ozma's COEWS file is known to the Consortium, which means it will be acquired eventually. The clock just got shorter.
This is the last session of the arc. The team came back from the outer system with knowledge that changes everything. This session is the moment where that knowledge meets the political world that will decide what to do with it. The Consortium's shipyard plan isn't just a hypercorp land-grab — it's a fleet being built to project force into the region where the Lithohex exists, where FK7 is, where the Semaphore has been pointing. An outer-system Consortium fleet, without knowing what it's sailing toward, is an extinction-risk delivery mechanism. That's what's in the data module. That's what's at stake. Not a diplomatic incident. Not a career. The next X-risk.
Whether the team treats this session as a heist, a social game, a moral argument, or a warning they're trying to deliver — the session's final beat should be the same: they leave knowing more about what's coming than anyone else in the solar system, and knowing that most of the solar system is not ready to hear it. The Semaphore's fourth arm is still pointing. Something else is out there. They haven't found it yet.
◈ GM NOTES — RUNNING THIS SCENARIO
This is the campaign's only pure social session. After nine sessions of outer-system horror, existential threat, and physical survival, the team is in a ballroom. The tonal inversion is the point. The stakes are identical — this is arguably the highest-stakes session in the arc — but the expression is entirely different. Let the players feel the dissonance. Flash will feel it most acutely. Use that.
The 0.113g gravity is a constant atmospheric note. Drinks float if spilled. People move with exaggerated grace or barely contained chaos. Designer creatures drift overhead. Everything is slightly wrong in a way that is aesthetically beautiful. This is a world that has rebuilt itself after civilisational collapse and made it look like a party.
The decryption key is the session's ethical centre. Kyo is not a villain. She is a competent professional who has been doing the right thing by the wrong system for a long time. The team asking her to hand over the key is asking her to commit career suicide, break the law, and trust strangers who won't tell her everything they know. The fact that they're right doesn't make it easy. Play this conversation with the weight it deserves. Don't let a single Persuade roll decide it.
If Luz reveals what the team found at FK7, Arrokoth, and KBO 2119-HR4 — and if MESA's theory about the Semaphore is shared — Kyo will sit with it for a long moment. Then: "If I give you this key, and this goes wrong, I lose everything. If I don't give you this key, and what you're describing is real —" She doesn't finish the sentence. She hands over the key or she doesn't. Either answer is valid. Either answer has consequences that carry forward.
The intelligence annex in Alciato's module reveals that a Consortium survey probe recorded the FK7 source object's transmission six months ago and has a survey mission en route — ETA fourteen weeks. This is a ticking clock the team didn't know existed. What they do with this information (tell Firewall, tell Grix and the Titanians, attempt to intercept the probe, do nothing) is a post-session consequence that shapes the next arc if there is one.
This is the last session of the arc. The team has been to the edge of the solar system and back. They've seen a TITAN fetch reconstruct itself, a dead colony, a silicon predator, and a temporal isolation weapon. They know something ancient near the sun has been trying to warn someone for years and nobody was listening. Now they're at a party. Let the ending be quiet. The last line belongs to whichever muse has been speaking all session: we bought time. I don't know how much. That's the right note. Not triumph. Not defeat. Just the weight of knowing what's out there, and still being alive, and the Semaphore's arm still pointing somewhere they haven't gone yet.
